Car Hire in the Algarve
without Surprises

By Ricardo Barbosa Founder & Editor, Portugal Travel Hub Updated: 5 October 2026

The Algarve motorway is now toll-free, deposits are still high and the best beaches have no bus. Everything you need to know before you collect your car at Faro.

Updated October 2026 12 min read Portugal Travel Hub
Photo: Thibault Mokuenko / Unsplash
Home Guides Car hire in the Algarve

Here is the truth about the Algarve that the brochures leave out: the beaches in the photographs are almost never next to a bus stop. Praia da Marinha, Arrifana, Bordeira, Cacela Velha, the Sagres headlands at sunset: in practice, you need a car to reach them. The train runs from Lagos to Vila Real de Santo António, but it stays well away from the cliff beaches. The buses serve the towns, not the coves.

I live in Portimão and spent around twenty years in Algarve hospitality, from Pine Cliffs to Carvoeiro Clube. A lot of guest conversations started the same way: someone arriving at reception annoyed about a charge they never saw coming at the car hire desk. It was rarely the rental company being dishonest. It was information nobody had given them before they booked.

This guide is that information. Updated in October 2026, with the rules that have changed (the biggest one: the Algarve motorway no longer has tolls) and the things you only learn by driving here every day.

01 · Tolls

What few rental desks will tell you: the A22 is now toll-free

The Via do Infante (A22), the motorway that crosses the Algarve from Lagos to the Guadiana bridge into Spain, stopped charging tolls on 1 January 2025 under Law 37/2024, which abolished tolls on several former SCUT motorways. The gantries are still there, but they no longer charge.

That changes a decision almost every visitor makes at the desk: the toll transponder (Via Verde or similar) that rental companies hire out by the day. Before 2025 it was close to essential. Today, if your trip stays inside the Algarve, you most likely don't need it.

When the transponder is still worth it
If you're driving to Lisbon on the A2, the Lisbon–Algarve toll is €23.80 for a standard car in 2026. The A2 has staffed or card-payment booths, so the device just saves you the queue. On other Portuguese motorways with electronic-only gantries, without a device you'll have to pay afterwards at a CTT post office or Payshop (between 2 and 15 working days after your trip, plus a small fee). If you're only in the Algarve, ask for the daily price and decide with this in mind.

A practical consequence locals already know: with the A22 free, there's no reason to cover long distances on the N125 coastal road. Use the A22 to get from one end of the region to the other, and keep the N125 for short hops between towns.

02 · Insurance and deposit

Insurance and deposit: where the money goes

The price you see online usually includes basic damage cover (CDW), but with an excess: the amount you pay yourself if the car is damaged. In Portugal it's typically between €500 and €1,500, and damage to tyres, glass and the underside of the car is often excluded.

To guarantee that excess, the company places a hold on your card. It isn't a charge, but the money is unavailable for the whole rental. For a real-world figure: one well-known company operating at Faro lists deposits between €900 and €2,100, depending on the car category, for customers who don't buy its own full cover.

Your three options, plainly

Basic cover only
  • Cheapest up front.
  • A large deposit held on your card.
  • Any scrape can cost you up to the full excess.
Full cover at the desk
  • The most convenient: it reduces or removes the excess and, with many companies, the deposit.
  • Usually the most expensive option per day.
  • Read the exclusions before you sign.
Third-party full cover (online)
  • For example, DiscoverCars Full Coverage, added when you book.
  • The desk still holds the full deposit.
  • If there's damage, you pay the rental company and claim it back afterwards (within 90 days, with the contract, damage report, invoice and proof of payment).
What I'd do
  • If you have a credit card with plenty of headroom: third-party cover online, and politely decline the desk's.
  • If you don't want your card blocked or to deal with claims: take the company's full cover and stop worrying.
Debit card? Check before you book
Most airport desks require a credit card in the main driver's name. Some companies accept debit cards; others refuse them or ask for a higher deposit. Prepaid cards are almost always refused. Check the specific company's terms before you pay, not when you land.
03 · Faro Airport

Collecting your car at Faro without losing the morning

There are two kinds of company at Faro Airport. Those with a desk in the terminal, in the arrivals area, with cars parked a few minutes' walk away. And off-airport companies, which meet you at a pick-up point and take you by shuttle to their office. The off-airport ones tend to be cheaper; the cost is time, and in August that can be a lot of it.

Three habits that save trouble:

Split up. If you're travelling with someone, one of you joins the desk queue while the other waits for the bags.

Film the car before you leave. A full walk-round video including wheels, windscreen and bumpers, plus a photo of the mileage and fuel gauge. Do the same when you return it.

Choose a full-to-full fuel policy. You collect it full and return it full. Options where you prepay a tank rarely make sense for a beach holiday. Leave time on your last day to fill up.

To compare terminal and off-airport companies, with deposit terms side by side, use a comparison site such as DiscoverCars at Faro. Before you pay, always open the chosen company's terms.

04 · Rules of the road

The rules that catch visitors out

Motorways (A22, A2)120 km/h (minimum 50 km/h)
Roundabouts
  • For the first exit, stay in the right-hand lane.
  • For later exits you may use the inner lanes, but you must move to the right-hand lane after passing the exit before yours.
  • Exiting straight from an inner lane is an offence, and one of the most common among visitors.
Alcohol
  • General limit: 0.5 g/l (lower than England's).
  • 0.2 g/l if you've held your licence for less than 3 years.
  • 1.2 g/l or more is a criminal offence. The GNR police run frequent checks in summer.
Children
  • Under-12s who are under 135 cm must use an approved child seat, in the back.
  • Book seats with the car: in August, desks can run out.
Phones and fines
  • No handheld phone use; hands-free or a single earpiece only.
  • A warning triangle and reflective vest are mandatory (check they're in the car).
  • For a roadside fine you must pay on the spot, at least the minimum amount. If you don't, your licence or the car documents can be held until you do.
05 · N125 and speed cameras

The N125: respect, not fear

The N125 links the coastal towns: roundabouts, restaurant entrances, pedestrian crossings and lorries. Road-safety data has ranked it among Portugal's most dangerous roads for years. You don't need to avoid it at all costs, just drive it calmly: limits that change every kilometre, overtakes that aren't worth it, and extra care in the evening.

There are fixed speed cameras on the N125 at Lagoa, Guia, Ferreiras and São Pedro (Faro), another on the Montenegro link road, and one on the A2 at Paderne (list published in 2025; more may be added). On the N125 camera stretches, the limit is 70 or 80 km/h.

06 · Parking

Parking: the real test

Driving in the Algarve is easy. Parking in August isn't. The car park at Praia da Marinha fills early on summer mornings, and once it's full the access road is sometimes closed. In Benagil village, parking in high summer is close to impossible. The local rule is simple: famous beaches before 9:30am or after 5pm.

Benagil: the rules changed in 2024
Since 2024 you can't land on the beach inside the cave or swim in, and unguided kayak hire is no longer allowed. Visit by boat or on a guided kayak tour from Benagil, Portimão or Armação de Pêra. Don't plan your morning around parking in Benagil.

In town centres, street parking is paid, and increasingly by phone. In Portimão you can use EasyPark, iParque, Telpark and Via Verde; in Loulé, Quarteira and Vilamoura, Via Verde Estacionar and iParque; in Lagos, EasyPark. Install one before you travel. Parking fines start at €30.

07 · When to book and what it costs

When to book and what you'll spend

Car hire in the Algarve is highly seasonal. Off-season prices can be very low; in July and August they multiply and the economy categories sell out. For summer, book two to three months ahead and pick a free-cancellation rate: if the price drops, cancel and rebook.

Fuel: in the week of 5 October 2026, unleaded 95 was around €2.07/litre and diesel around €2.14–2.20/litre (unusually, diesel is currently dearer). Budget brands are usually a few cents cheaper. Always check the fuel type on the filler cap before you fill up.

Electric cars: Portugal's public Mobi.E network has more than 16,000 charging points, and at many of them you can pay directly with a bank card. If you hire an EV, check the cable and range at collection.

Young drivers: the usual minimum age is 21, and drivers under 25 normally pay an extra daily fee.

08 · Licences and Spain

Driving licences and day trips to Spain

EU licences are valid as they are. A UK photocard licence is too. US and Brazilian licences are recognised in Portugal, but some rental desks still ask for an International Driving Permit: if you have one, bring it, because it avoids arguments. It's only valid together with your original licence.

Seville is around two hours from Faro, and the temptation is real. Taking a hire car across the border needs the company's prior permission and usually a cross-border fee (often €60–150). Declare it when you book. Crossing without permission can leave you uninsured.

The final checklist, to save on your phone

Before you book
  • Free cancellation and a full-to-full fuel policy.
  • The deposit amount, and whether your card type is accepted.
  • Terminal desk or shuttle: is the saving worth the time?
  • Child seats and any additional driver booked now.
At the desk
  • Decline extras you didn't decide on beforehand (including the toll transponder, if you're staying in the Algarve).
  • Read the excess and exclusions in the contract.
  • A full video of the car before you drive off.
On the road
  • The A22 for long distances; the N125 only for short hops.
  • Roundabouts: only exit from the right-hand lane.
  • Famous beaches before 9:30am.
At return
  • A full tank, and keep the fuel receipt.
  • Another video of the car, in the company's car park.
  • Ask for written confirmation that the car was returned undamaged.

Frequently asked questions

Do I really need a car in the Algarve?

If you want to see the cliff beaches, the Vicentine Coast or the inland villages, yes. If you're staying in one town and using the town beach, you can manage without.

Does the A22 (Via do Infante) have tolls?

No. Tolls on the A22 were abolished on 1 January 2025. The A2 motorway to Lisbon still has tolls.

How much is the deposit to hire a car at Faro?

It depends on the company and car category. Without the company's own full cover, holds from a few hundred euros to over €2,000 on the main driver's credit card are common.

Can I hire a car with a debit card?

Some companies accept them, many don't, and prepaid cards are almost always refused. Check the company's terms before you pay.

Do I need an International Driving Permit?

Not with an EU licence or a UK photocard licence. US and Brazilian licences are recognised, but carrying an IDP avoids problems at the desk.
